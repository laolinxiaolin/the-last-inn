# 06 — Session Structure

## One session = one evening at the inn

Target: 15-20 minutes. The player can always close early — closing early is never punished.

## The arc of an evening

### Opening (1-2 min)
- You light the fire. (One click. It's a small ritual.)
- Check the cellar: the batch you set last session is ready. A new keg to tap.
- Check the board: new rumors, new letters, the quest board's state.
- The window shows the evening — weather, season, what the world looks like tonight.

### The evening (10-15 min)
- Guests arrive in waves. The door bell announces each one.
- Between guests: quiet beats. The fire. The rain. You can pour yourself one.
- Returns arrive *between* guests too — the door creaks and you look up, and it might be anyone.
- Core verbs: pour, listen, ask, judge, equip, send.

### Closing (1-2 min)
- Last call. The room thins out.
- One decision: what batch to set fermenting for next session.
- The fire dies to embers. The inn settles. Auto-save at dawn.

## Time

- Quests take days. When the inn closes, days pass in a quiet montage — the inn seen across weather and light, the seasons turning in the window.
- The game tracks a calendar. Seasons change the window, the guests, the quests, the ale available.
- Winter is real: fewer travelers, harder nights, the dark closer. And winter is beautiful.

## The door bell

Every creak matters. The bell is the game's one instrument of suspense — a return, a guest, a courier, or just the wind. The game controls the rhythm: easy quests return fast, dire quests return slow, some never return. The player learns to read the bell: *that was a light step. That was a heavy one.*

## Quiet nights

Some nights nobody comes. This is a feature, not a failure state.

On a quiet night: the fire, the rain, Garrick in the corner, the wall of swords. You can talk to whoever's there, or just sit. The game holds the space. A middle-aged man knows that an empty inn can be company.

## "You look tired."

Sometimes, at closing, the game says it — in the innkeeper's own voice, in a moment of quiet: *"You look tired. The fire'll keep."*

The game can suggest stopping. It never punishes rest. The game is the innkeeper. The player is the tired traveler. Be kind to him — that's the whole design.

## Save & quit

- Auto-save at dawn, every session.
- The quit button is diegetic: you close the inn.
- Pause is instant, anywhere, one button. The world waits. It's a patient game.

## Session length targets

- Act 1 (the hearth): sessions 1-10, ~20 min each — establishing, comedy-heavy
- Act 2 (the dark creeps): sessions 10-25, ~20 min — threads resolve, backstory drips
- Act 3 (the door): sessions 25+, ~20 min — the endgame, the final evenings
- After the ending: The Embers — endless quiet nights, no quests, no stakes. Just the inn.
