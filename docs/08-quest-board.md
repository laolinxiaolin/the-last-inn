# 08 — The Quest Board

## Sources of quests

- **Rumors** — overheard at the bar, bought with good pours
- **Stories** — returned adventurers bring news, and news becomes jobs
- **The region** — villages send pleas, the desperate come to the door
- **Letters** — mailed requests, sometimes years old
- **The regulars** — they know what's actually happening out there

## Quest anatomy

Every quest has:

- **A name that undersells it** — "A Small Problem at the Mill." "A Question of Caravan." "A Door in the Tower."
- **A tier** — Small / Concerning / Dire / The Last. The tier names get more honest as the world darkens.
- **Apparent danger** — what the board says. Almost always wrong, slightly.
- **True danger** — what's really there. Never told. The player reads it from rumors, ale, and their own judgment.
- **A truth** — most quests are not what they seem. The mill is a negotiation. The caravan is a warning. The tower is a door.
- **Requirements** — implied, not stated: some quests need a party, a quiet voice, a strong arm, or someone the dark hasn't touched yet.
- **Rewards** — coin, ale ingredients, gear, rumors, reputation, and *knowledge*. The best reward is a rumor that makes the next quest readable.

## Tiers (in-world language)

| Tier | Board says | Actually |
|---|---|---|
| Small | "a small problem" | usually small, sometimes a knife in the dark |
| Concerning | "a concerning matter" | the first real risks |
| Dire | "a dire need" | deaths are possible and the player can feel it |
| The Last | "the door" | the endgame quests, assembled by the player's choices |

## The party system

Some quests need two or three. This is where judgment gets deep:

- **Synergy** — the careful dwarf keeps the eager kid alive. The bard's tongue and the woman's blade open doors neither could alone.
- **Friction** — the bard scares easily; the woman has no patience for cowards; the kid worships people who'll get him killed.
- **Loyalty** — a loyal adventurer comes home. A mercenary with no loyalty deserts with the reward. Read it before you send it.

## The wall is the armory

The museum is also the inventory. The swords of the dead and retired hang on the wall — and you can take one down and give it to someone going out.

*"Take my friend's sword. It's served me well."*

Lending a dead man's blade is a moment, every time. If the borrower comes back, the sword comes back — and the story of it has grown. If they don't, you hang it again, and the wall has two names now.

## How outcomes are decided

No dice, no numbers. Outcomes follow real logic the player can learn to feel:

**Adventurer (skill + loyalty + temperament + luck) + fit + equipment + ale + party synergy vs. quest (true danger + truth)**

The player is never told these values. They're *felt* — through tells, through ale, through rumors, through a lifetime of reading people.

## Failure is soft — and fair

- Failures are funny, or sad, or both. Nobody dies without the player having had a chance to see it coming.
- **The golden rule: the player is never surprised by a death they couldn't have seen coming.** The game always leaves tells. If someone dies, the player will be able to look back and see exactly where the truth was — usually on the bar in front of them.

## The board and the world

As the world darkens, the board changes:

- Fewer volunteers. Harsher quests. Names that stop underselling.
- The desperate come with new kinds of jobs: escort the refugees, carry the supplies, bring word to a village that won't last the winter.
- And eventually, one quest appears that the player didn't post, didn't hear, didn't ask for. A single line, in a hand they almost recognize:

> *"The door is open."*
