# 07 — Guests

## Philosophy

**No random spawns.** Every guest is an authored character from a curated cast (target: 40-60 for the full game). The inn is a stage — every entrance is a scene, every character has a story arc that spans sessions.

## The anatomy of a guest

Every guest has:

- **Identity** — name (sometimes earned), origin, what they claim to be
- **Visible** — appearance, gear, demeanor, how they hold their ale. What the player *sees*
- **Hidden** — Skill, Loyalty, Luck, Temperament. What the player must *infer*
- **A secret** — something true that they're not telling
- **An agenda** — why they're really here
- **Ale behavior** — what they drink, how they drink, what loosens them
- **Relationship state** — tabs, debts, trust, history with the inn
- **A story arc** — how they change across sessions, whether they come back

## Guest types

- **Regulars** — the cast that lives at the inn's edge of the world: Garrick, Grib, the returning heroes. The inn is their home too.
- **Travelers** — one-night scenes, hand-written but not recurring. They carry rumors and quests.
- **The desperate** — as the world darkens, refugees and the broken come to the door. They don't want quests. They want shelter.

## The first-night roster

Six guests. The player's first evening. Each one is written to test the tone, teach a system, and start a thread.

---

### 1. RENN — the kid

**Seen:** Seventeen. Farm boy. A sword far too big for him, borrowed from the blacksmith and never returned. Talks too fast, laughs too loud, gulps his ale like water. Eyes too big for his face.

**Hidden:** Skill 3/10. Loyalty 10/10. Luck 8/10 (the game's favorite irony: the terrible ones with luck survive). Temperament: eager.

**His truth:** He's not running away from anything. He's the one the mill village *sent*. The goblins moved into the old mill; the village is afraid; he volunteered to fetch a hero. All they had was him.

**Ale:** Drinks the Common like water. The Dark makes him cry — it's the first time someone's been kind without wanting anything.

**Quest fit:** The mill (it's his village — the perfect first quest). Bad on anything dire. Never let him go alone somewhere you wouldn't go.

**Arc:** Comes back a man. Becomes a regular. The player's small absolution. If he dies — the courier's sword is the game's first gut-punch, and it's the player's fault, and the game forgives them, and the wall remembers.

**Tone note:** The comedy is his bravado. The engine underneath is that he's a child doing an adult's errand, and the game loves him for it.

---

### 2. KELD — the dwarf

**Seen:** Sits in the corner. Says nothing. Scarred knuckles, one finger missing — the hands of a man who's killed things and doesn't want to anymore. Nurses one drink all night. Watches the door.

**Hidden:** Skill 9/10. Loyalty 7/10 (absolute, once earned). Luck 2/10 (the world owes him). Temperament: stone.

**His truth:** He's a tomb-guard whose charge is dead. He failed. The thing he guarded is loose — and it came this way. He's not here to drink. He's here to warn, and to wait.

**Ale:** The Dark. He never asks for it. When you pour it without being asked, that's the first crack — a single nod. He says one thing on night one: *"The tower's not the problem. The tower's a door."*

**Quest fit:** The tower. Send him too early, alone, uninformed — he comes back with a scar and a sentence: *"You should have waited."* (Soft lesson, not a punishment.)

**Arc:** Opens up over sessions. One sentence about his daughter, said once, at closing. Then never again. He's the game's gravity.

**Tone note:** The gravity. When the room gets silly, he's the still point the comedy bounces off.

---

### 3. THE WOMAN IN GREY

**Seen:** Hooded. Pays in silver that's *too new*. Drinks the Strong — she wants to feel nothing. Never eats. Watches everyone the way the innkeeper does — she reads people too.

**Hidden:** Skill 8/10. Loyalty 3/10 (to anyone else — 10/10 to her purpose). Luck 5/10. Temperament: sealed.

**Her truth:** She was the caravan's escort. She's the only survivor. Everyone died; she took what was left and walked, and the silver is guilt. She's not a villain — she's a survivor trying not to feel it. And the thing that took the caravan is in the tower, and she can't face it alone, and she will hire anyone to go with her.

**Ale:** The Strong, first. The Dark, eventually. The Bitter makes her leave — if you pour it, she walks out, and the thread waits a season.

**Quest fit:** The tower (she knows it), the deep road. Never the mill — that's beneath her, and she'd be cruel to it.

**Arc:** The caravan reveal — the player pieces it together from the silver, from Fenwick's silence, from the too-even story she tells. Confront her, trust her, or smoke her out: all valid, all different threads.

**Tone note:** The mystery. She's the player's mirror — another reader of people, one who chose to seal herself.

---

### 4. FENWICK — the bard

**Seen:** A hat that is a crime. Talks constantly. Claims to have slain a dragon — he hid in a barrel, the dragon tripped over the barrel, everyone assumed. Orders the cheapest thing and complains about it. Sings to the fire.

**Hidden:** Skill 5/10. Loyalty 2/10 (but curious — bards need material). Luck 7/10. Temperament: wind.

**His truth:** He was the caravan's hired entertainer. He's the one who hid in the barrel while everyone died. The dragon story *is* the barrel story — his trauma is disguised as comedy, and he's the funniest person in the room because he's hiding the worst thing.

**Ale:** The Sweet, claims he hates it. Drunk, he tells the truth in the key of a joke, and nobody laughs.

**Quest fit:** Never alone on dangerous jobs. Excellent at parley, negotiation, information — the talking quests. With a strong partner, he's fine; he deserts only if he's scared, and he's scared of the tower.

**Arc:** He and the woman in grey know each other from the caravan. Neither admits it. The barrel comes out eventually — and the game's funniest character has the saddest story, and that's the tone.

**Tone note:** The comedy hiding the wound. He's the test: the player must learn to read past the performance.

---

### 5. GARRICK — the old hero

**Seen:** Old, retired, drinks in the corner every night. Tab so old it's a museum piece. Watches *you* more than the door. Laughs at the kid's jokes too hard — he's laughing at the old days.

**Hidden:** Skill 9/10 (the old dog still has it). Loyalty 10/10. Luck: alive (that's the whole thing). Temperament: iron.

**His truth:** He was your adventuring companion. He was *there* on your last quest — the one with the kid. He's not retired; he followed you. He counts your drinks to make sure you're still here. He's the only person who knows who you were, and he's afraid of what you'll do when the dark reaches the door.

**Ale:** The Common. Always. Never asks for anything. When you pour him a Dark unprompted, he holds it a long time. That's the moment.

**Quest fit:** "I'm retired." He won't take quests. Late game, when the door opens, he's the one who goes with you — no question, no speech, just standing up.

**Arc:** The reveal of why he's really here. The final evening. Two old men counting each other's drinks, neither willing to say it.

**Tone note:** The quiet love between old men that neither will say aloud. The game's most important relationship.

---

### 6. GRIB — the goblin *(arrives night two)*

**Seen:** Small, polite, pays in advance, compliments the ale, writes thank-you notes. Keeps his hands on the table where you can see them — he knows what people think of goblins. The best guest you've ever had.

**Hidden:** Skill 7/10 (an engineer — goblin machinery). Loyalty 10/10. Luck 6/10. Temperament: gentle.

**His truth:** He's the mill goblins' ambassador. They're not raiders — they're refugees who sheltered in a derelict mill. No one was harmed. The village is afraid, and fear is becoming a war, and he was sent to the one neutral ground in the world to ask for help before it starts.

**Ale:** The Common, with gratitude. The Sweet, with joy — he's never been served anything sweet.

**Quest fit:** He *is* the mill quest's truth. Meet him before you send anyone to "clear the mill," and the quest becomes a parley. Send Renn to clear it first, and Renn comes back conflicted: they're families. The player learns the game's first big lesson — **the quest is never what the board says it is.**

**Arc:** Grib becomes a regular — the bridge between village and goblins. Late game, when the dark comes, the goblins are the ones who remember the inn's kindness and bring supplies. Hospitality is magic, and here's the proof.

**Tone note:** The kindness. He's the game's thesis: read past the story, and you'll find a person.
