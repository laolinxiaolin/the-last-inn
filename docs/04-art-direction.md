# 04 — Art Direction

## The problem

The player stares at one room for forty hours. The art can't just be decoration — **the inn itself has to be the most interesting character in the game.**

## The headline: the inn is a living museum of your own playthrough

Every sword that comes back gets hung on the wall. Every hero who retires leaves a keepsake behind. Every death is a weapon you keep. By session 30, the walls are covered with the history of *your* game — and you remember every single piece. The player walks past a notched sword and thinks: *that's the kid from session 8.*

Their choices literally decorate the room. The inn becomes a physical record of the playthrough. This is both a systems decision and an art direction decision. (See [14 — Art Bible](14-art-bible.md) for the asset spec.)

## The palette — two lights telling the story

- **Amber firelight** = the inn. Warmth, safety, comedy. The hearth is the heart of the picture.
- **Blue windowlight** = the world outside. Adventure, danger, the unknown. One window, and through it: rain, snow, fog, the treeline creeping closer as the world darkens.

The balance between the two IS the game's mood meter. Early game: fire blazing, window bright. Late game: the fire is lower, the window is all you can see, and the dark is at the glass. No UI popups. The player just *feels* it change.

## The POV — you are never on screen

The camera sits behind the bar. You see the guests across the wood, the fire beyond them. You're hands, a shoulder, a tankard sliding across the bar.

Practical (no player animation needed) and a statement: *you are the fixed point. The world comes to you.* It also makes every guest entrance a framed shot — the door swings, rain blows in, a silhouette fills the frame.

## The chosen style: 90s painterly anime

**The look: anime character design sitting on hand-painted storybook backgrounds.**

- **Characters:** 90s anime conventions — bold clean outlines, big expressive eyes, exaggerated reaction faces, cel shading with limited shadow steps. The expression language carries the comedy: the bard's panic, the kid's bravado, the dwarf's single raised eyebrow.
- **Backgrounds:** hand-painted, watercolor/gouache texture, visible brushwork, warm muted palette. The atmosphere carries the melancholy: the empty chair, the low fire, rain on the window.
- **The contrast rule:** characters are cel-flat and clean; backgrounds are painterly and textured. In 90s anime, that contrast made characters *pop* out of painted worlds — we keep it deliberately. It's also why this style works for the game: faces are instantly readable, rooms are instantly warm.

**Reference anchors (mood reference, for the art bible — see [14](14-art-bible.md)):**
- Record of Lodoss War (1990 OVA) — fantasy taverns, character design, painterly backgrounds. *The primary reference.*
- Early Studio Ghibli backgrounds (Nausicaä, Kiki's Delivery Service) — painterly light and atmosphere.
- Cowboy Bebop (1998) — 90s character polish and framing.
- Slayers (1995) — comedic fantasy energy.

**Why this style:**
1. It's the aesthetic of the target player's youth — this is the demographic that grew up on 80s/90s OVAs
2. Anime faces carry comedy instantly; painted rooms carry melancholy beautifully. We need both (70/30 tone)
3. It's a distinct, coherent look that holds up across 40-60 sessions

**Rejected:**
- Modern glossy anime (Genshin/Honkai style) — too bright, too young, fights the tone
- Pure storybook painted — warmer, but loses the expression language
- 3D cel-shaded — unnecessary production cost for a one-room game; AI tools don't generate rigged 3D anyway

## The audio is part of the art

Fire crackle. Rain on the roof. Tankards. Creaking wood. A low, warm ambient bed that can fall silent at the dark moments — the empty sound of an inn that's waiting for someone who isn't coming back.

Sound is half the pub feeling, and it's cheap to do well. Full spec in [15 — Audio Brief](15-audio-brief.md).

## Readability is a design value

Parchment-and-wood UI, huge type, warm colors. No thin fonts, no tiny icons, no blue-on-blue. A tired man at midnight with the lights off should never squint. Readability is baked into the art direction.

## The pitch in one image

> A hand-painted 90s anime inn at dusk, seen from behind the bar: cel-shaded characters with big expressive eyes in the amber firelight, a window of blue night, watercolor walls slowly filling with the swords of everyone you've sent out — and everyone who came home.
