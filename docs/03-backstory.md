# 03 — The Player's Backstory

The backstory is the spine of the game. It isn't plot — it's the *engine* of the emotional systems: the returns, the judgment, the ale.

## Options considered

### A. The Rumor
The world says you retired after a legendary victory. The truth: your greatest victory wasn't a victory. The "dark lord" you slew was a man — framed, desperate, with a family. You killed him, the world cheered, and you've never been able to hear a bard sing about it without drinking alone.
*Gives:* moral weight. *Costs:* too heavy at the center for the pub-comedy tone.

### B. The Knees
There was no tragedy. You were a great hero, and then one morning your back hurt, the kid in the sparring ring was faster than you, and you realized you'd been *lucky* — every hero is just a kid who didn't die yet. So you quit while you were ahead.
The world refuses to believe this. Dramatic rumors circulate (cursed! betrayed! lost a duel to a demon!) and you keep trying to tell people the truth — *"I have a bad knee, ask the miller's wife"* — and no one believes you.
*Gives:* maximum relatability. *Costs:* internal, not external. Needs a mystery to hang on.

### C. The Deal
A stranger saved your life when you were young — at the price that you'd never fight again. Endgame: the stranger comes to collect, and you choose whether to break the bargain.
*Gives:* a spine, a villain, a climax. *Costs:* the least *real* of the three.

## Chosen: THE MUNDANE TRUTH, WITH ONE THING YOU'VE NEVER SAID

A blend of A and B.

**The public story (the comedy):** everyone thinks you have a mysterious tragic past, and the truth is you just got old and quit before you got someone killed. Dry, funny, relatable.

**The hidden layer (the gut-punch):** your last quest wasn't a milk run. You lost someone out there — a kid, just like the ones who come to your door now. Not to a monster. To *your mistake*. You misjudged a situation, sent him in, and he didn't come back. You couldn't face watching another one die. That's the real reason you stopped.

## Why this connects everything

- **Why you're so good at reading adventurers** — you've been reading them for forty years, looking for the ones who'll die
- **Why the returns hit so hard** — every kid who comes back is a small absolution
- **Why the courier with the sword is the worst moment in the game** — you've received that sword before
- **Why you never fight anymore** — not because you can't. Because you're afraid to lose another one

## How the player learns it — the drip-feed

The truth is revealed the way you'd learn it about a real man — in drips, over many sessions:

- **Session 5:** you find an old boot behind the bar and hold it too long before throwing it out
- **Session 12:** a bard sings a song you hum along to — and stops mid-verse, because you've gone quiet
- **Session 20:** a kid walks in with the same damn sword, the same damn grin, and you have to *choose* whether to send him out anyway

## The whole game in one line

> The inn is where you hide from the thing that ended you — and every night, you open the door anyway.
