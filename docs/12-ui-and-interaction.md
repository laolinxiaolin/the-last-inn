# 12 — UI & Interaction

## One stick, one button

The entire game is playable with one hand. No menus as screens — everything is diegetic.

| System | Diegetic form |
|---|---|
| Menu | The bar |
| Inventory | The pours in front of you |
| Quest log | The board behind you |
| World map | The window |
| Codex / lore | The wall of swords |
| Character sheet | The regulars at the bar |
| Save | Auto-save at dawn |
| Quit | Close the inn |

## The bar is the hub

The camera sits behind the bar, and the world is arranged around you:

- **Left and right along the bar** — guests, one at a time, in conversation
- **Behind you** — the quest board, the cellar stairs
- **The end of the bar** — the window
- **In front of you** — the pours. The ale you've tapped is right there. Choosing is a glance, not a menu

## Dialogue

- 2–4 options, huge text, no timers
- **There are no wrong options** — every option is valid, they just cost different truths. The Bitter costs the thread. The Dark costs the cask. Saying nothing costs the moment
- Silence is an option. Sometimes the best read is to pour and wait

## The camera

Slow. Warm. The game never rushes the player.

- Pulls in for the pour — the game's one ceremony
- Pulls in for toasts
- Sits back for the room, the fire, the rain
- Holds still at the dark moments. Doesn't flinch. Lets the player sit in it

## Accessibility (baked in, not bolted on)

- Huge type, warm palette, parchment and wood. No thin fonts, no tiny icons, no blue-on-blue
- One-handed controller. Pause instantly, anywhere — the world waits
- The game can suggest stopping: *"You look tired. The fire'll keep."* Never punishes rest
- No flashing, no motion that demands attention, no text that disappears

## The pour (the one action that matters)

The deepest input in the game is choosing a glass, and it should *feel* like it:

- The camera settles. The firelight catches the glass. The ale sounds right — that low, thick, satisfying pour
- The slide across the wood. The nod. The moment
- A tired man gets to do *one* satisfying thing, over and over, and it's this

## The tutorial is the kid

No tutorial screens. The first night teaches everything:

- Renn arrives — you pour, you listen, you ask, you read
- Keld says one sentence — you learn that silence is information
- The woman pays in too-new silver — you learn to notice
- Fenwick sings — you learn that stories are performance
- Garrick counts your drinks — you learn the game is watching you too
- You send the kid to the mill — you learn to wait

## What the player should never have to do

- Squint
- Remember a control
- Read a stat
- Race anything
- Feel rushed
- Lose progress to a mistake made while tired

## The one non-diegetic thing

The pause menu, which is a single page: *Resume / Close the inn (save & quit) / You look tired (suggests stopping).* Three buttons. That's the whole menu.
