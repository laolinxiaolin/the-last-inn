# 13 — Narrative Arc & Endings

## The shape

Three acts, told across ~40-60 sessions of 15-20 minutes each. The game is finite — it *ends*, beautifully — and then the Embers.

## Act 1 — The Hearth (sessions 1-10)

The pub comedy. The cast assembles:

- Night one: Renn, Keld, the woman in grey, Fenwick, Garrick. The first pour. The first send
- Night two: Grib arrives. The mill truth lands. The player learns the quest is never what the board says
- The first returns. The first soft-fail legend. The warmth of the inn
- **The first loss** — somewhere in this act, the player misjudges someone and the courier comes. The game forgives. The wall grows. The player learns what the game is *made of*
- Winter's first arrival. The window changes

**Act 1 tone:** the warmest the game ever is. The comedy is at full strength. The gut-punches are single, and they teach.

## Act 2 — The Dark Creeps (sessions 10-25)

The threads resolve, the world thins:

- The caravan truth: the woman's silver, Fenwick's barrel, the too-even story. Confront, trust, or smoke her out — the thread ends three ways
- Keld opens up. One sentence about his daughter. The tower's warning becomes the tower's *key*
- The backstory drips: the boot behind the bar, the song stopped mid-verse, a kid with the same damn sword
- Garrick's truth begins to surface: he wasn't retired. He followed you
- The mill resolution pays off — goblins and village, or a scar that lasts
- The comedy goes quiet, slowly. The funny guests thin out. The window changes faster

**Act 2 tone:** the shift is invisible until it isn't. The player realizes one night: *when did the road get this quiet?*

## Act 3 — The Door (sessions 25+)

The endgame:

- The tower. Keld's knowledge, the woman's relic, the bard's truth, Garrick's patience — the pieces the player has been assembling all game come together
- The locked door in the cellar becomes the center of the game. The inn is a capstone. The last inn because it's the last seal
- The world outside the window is nearly dark
- The last evenings: the regulars are still there. The fire is lower. On the bar: the choice

## The endings (candidates)

The endgame is a decision, not a battle. No stats, no combat — the trial is the player's own history. The door opens, and what's inside is what the player fears, and what the player fears is *failure* — so the door shows him the kid. The boy he lost is there, waiting: *"You sent me in. You never came for me."*

The way out is the one thing the player's been avoiding all game: honesty. *"I was afraid. I'm sorry. I'm here now."*

From there, three endings — all valid, none moralized:

### The Sealer
The inn is sealed with the door. The fire goes out. The region slowly recovers — the window brightens, place by place. You walk away from the inn you built... or stay in the dark, keeping watch. Ambiguous. Quiet. The walls stay full.

### The One Who Went In
You break your vow. You take the sword off the wall — your own, or Garrick's, or the kid's — and you go in. Garrick stands up without a word and goes with you. You come home old, whole, and *finished*. The inn keeps burning. The player stops being the one who sends others.

### The One Who Sent Them
You send the adventurers in. They come back changed, carrying something of the dark with them. The inn endures. You're still behind the bar, still pouring, still reading. This isn't the "bad" ending — it's the ending of the man who stayed, and the game honors it.

## The Embers

After any ending: a quiet mode with no quests, no stakes, no board. Just the inn, the regulars who remain, the fire, the rain. The player can visit forever — or close the inn one last time and walk away, and the game lets them go.

The game that lets you retire. The last kindness.

## Design notes for the ending

- The trial being a *conversation* honors the game's core verb — the player talks their way through the worst thing, the way they've talked their way through everything
- The kid's lines should mirror the player's own playthrough: what the player did with quests, with ale, with trust gets reflected back
- The endings must not moralize. The game has been kind for forty sessions; it doesn't get preachy at the end
- The final image in every ending: the fire. It's the whole game in one light
