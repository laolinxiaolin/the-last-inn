# 10 — World State

## The region

The world outside the window has places, and each place has a life:

- **The mill village** — Renn's home. Where the goblin question lives.
- **The crossroads** — trade, travelers, rumor. The inn's lifeline.
- **The forest** — between the inn and everything. It gets closer, over time.
- **The deep road** — where the caravan died. Where things go that don't come back.
- **The tower** — not the problem. The tower is a door.
- **Beyond** — the dark.

## State, not stats

Each place has a state, never a number: **stable → strained → failing → fallen.**

The player never sees the state. The player sees:

- Lights in the valley at night — fewer, every season
- The treeline — closer in the window
- The road — emptier, then desperate
- Snow that doesn't melt
- The bell — quieter

**The window is the world map.** No UI. The player reads the world the way the innkeeper would: by looking out at it.

## How quests move the world

- A success steadies a place. The mill village brightens — you see the difference in the window.
- A failure dims it. Soft failures just cost the inn's mood; hard failures cost the world.
- The desperate quests (escort the refugees, carry the supplies) are the most important ones in the game — they don't pay well, but they hold the line. The game never says this. The window shows it.

## The rhythm of the dark

The dark creeps *slowly*. One step every several sessions. The player shouldn't notice for a long time — then should feel it like weather: *when did the road get this quiet?*

- Darker world → fewer travelers, more refugees, harsher quests
- The comedy goes quiet. The funny guests thin out. The ones who come are tired
- The inn's own fire — the player notices it's lower, and can stoke it (a choice, not a chore)
- The ale runs scarce. The House Ale tastes different. *"There's grief in this one."*

## Guest flow

The world state shapes who comes to the door:

- **Stable world:** travelers, adventurers, the comedy of the road
- **Strained world:** fewer travelers, more purpose — people come *for* something
- **Failing world:** the desperate. They don't want quests. They want shelter. The inn becomes what its name says it is
- **Fallen places:** their people don't come anymore. The window is empty where they were

## The locked door

The cellar has a door. It's always been there. The inn is built on it — the cellar was never a cellar. It's a capstone.

This is the secret under everything:

- The dark gathers because of what's behind the door
- The inn is the *last* inn because it's the last seal
- The tower is a door — and so is the cellar. The world has more than one threshold
- The player didn't choose this inn by accident. It chose him. (Or: he drifted here, and only learns later what he's been sitting on. The design should keep this ambiguous until Act 3.)

The door doesn't open by itself. The player's choices assemble the circumstances across the whole game: the tower quest (Keld's knowledge), the caravan thread (the woman's relic), the bard's truth, Garrick's patience, the state of the world. When the dark finally presses hard enough — or when the player opens it deliberately — the endgame begins.

## What the door is for

The door is the endgame, and the endgame is a *decision*, not a battle. The last evenings:

- The world outside the window is nearly dark
- The inn is quiet. The regulars are still there
- On the bar: the choice
